# Day 22 – Expressing Genuine Appreciation and Recognition

**Focus today:** Notice what someone does well and say it clearly and specifically. Genuine appreciation strengthens relationships and motivates people. Recognition makes people feel seen and valued.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. Let your pace stay steady and your face open as you speak.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising expressing genuine appreciation and recognition.

Appreciation is powerful when it is specific and sincere.

When I name what someone did well, they feel valued.

That builds connection and encourages more of the good work.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin with a simple idea.

People do their best work when they know it is noticed.

Then I practise a short appreciation.

Someone stayed late to finish a careful review that caught an important error.

I say: “Thank you for the careful review last night. Catching that error made a real difference for the team.”

I say it once at normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, letting the words feel warm and specific.

The second version feels more genuine.

Good appreciation names the action and the impact.

It does not stay vague like “good job.”

People feel seen when the recognition is clear.

I keep my voice warm and steady.

I do not rush past the thanks.

I let it settle and then stay quiet for a moment.

If I notice I kept it too general, I pause and add one specific detail.

No long apology. Just a clearer recognition and I continue.

For example: “The way you explained the options helped everyone decide more easily. That clarity was really helpful.”

The conversation stays positive and encouraging.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that expressing appreciation makes me look more generous and present.

People trust speakers who can celebrate others openly.

If my energy starts to fade, I soften my shoulders and smile a little as I name what worked.

The same appreciation can still feel sincere.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that expressing genuine appreciation is a skill I can practise every day.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for any places where the appreciation stayed general. Read those lines again, adding one specific action and one clear impact. Notice how the recognition feels more meaningful.
