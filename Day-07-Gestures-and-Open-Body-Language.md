# Day 7 – Gestures & Open Body Language

**Focus today:** Let your hands and body support your words. Stay open. Move with purpose. Look calm and welcoming.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Keep your shoulders soft. Let your hands move naturally as you speak. Smile when it feels true.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising gestures and open body language.

My voice is only part of the message.

The rest comes from how I hold myself and how I use my hands.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders relaxed. Chest open.

My hands rest easily at my sides for a moment.

I look into my own eyes in the mirror.

I do not cross my arms.

I do not hide my hands in my pockets.

I stay open.

When I speak about something clear and simple, my hands can stay still.

When I want to emphasise a point, one hand can rise gently and open.

Like this.

I do not wave or point sharply.

I let the movement be small and controlled.

Open palms feel welcoming.

They show I have nothing to hide.

If I talk about two ideas, I can use both hands — one for each side.

Then I bring them back together when the ideas connect.

The movement matches the meaning.

I practise a short sentence with a gesture.

“I am ready to contribute.”

I say it once with my hands still.

Then I say it again and open one hand slightly on the word “contribute.”

The gesture makes the sentence feel stronger without getting louder.

In interviews I will remember this.

I will keep my hands visible.

I will lean forward just a little when I listen.

I will smile with my eyes as well as my mouth.

People trust what they see as much as what they hear.

If I feel nervous, my body can still look calm.

I drop my shoulders.

I uncross anything that has tightened.

I place my feet firmly and take one slow breath.

Then I continue.

Open body language is not acting.

It is the choice to stay relaxed and available.

It tells the other person: I am here with you.

I look at myself again.

I raise one hand gently as I say the next line.

“I am learning to speak with my whole self.”

I bring the hand back down.

I notice how the movement felt natural instead of forced.

If a gesture feels awkward, I simplify it.

Small is better than busy.

Still is better than stiff.

I finish this practice with a full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

My posture is open.

My hands are free.

I am learning to let my body support my words.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself on video. Watch with the sound off first. Notice whether your hands stayed hidden or moved with purpose. Then watch with sound and see if the gestures matched the words. Practise the same two sentences again, deliberately opening your hands on the key words.
