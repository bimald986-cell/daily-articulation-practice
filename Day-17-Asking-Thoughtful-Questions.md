# Day 17 – Asking Thoughtful Questions and Inviting Dialogue

**Focus today:** Ask questions that open the conversation instead of closing it. Thoughtful questions show interest, invite others in, and make the exchange feel balanced and human.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. Let your pace stay steady and your face open as you speak.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising asking thoughtful questions and inviting dialogue.

Speaking is not only about what I say.

It is also about the space I create for others.

A good question does both.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin with a simple idea.

People feel valued when we ask something real.

Then I add a question.

What has been most useful for you lately?

I say the question once at normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, letting the words land and leaving a short pause at the end.

The pause shows that I am genuinely waiting for an answer.

Open questions usually start with what, how, or why in a gentle way.

They give the other person room to think and speak.

I practise one short shift.

Closed version: “Did you like the meeting?”

Open version: “What stood out to you in the meeting?”

I say both. I notice which one feels more inviting.

In conversations this helps me.

People stay more engaged when they know their thoughts are welcome.

I keep my voice warm and steady.

I do not rush the question.

I let it settle and then stay quiet for a moment.

If I notice I have been speaking for a while, I pause and turn it outward.

No long apology. Just a clear question and I wait.

For example: “That is the idea I wanted to share… What is your take on it?”

The conversation stays balanced and useful.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that thoughtful questions make me look more confident and interested in others.

People trust speakers who can listen as well as they speak.

If my energy starts to fade, I soften my shoulders and lift my voice slightly on the question itself.

The invitation can still feel warm.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that inviting dialogue is a skill I can practise every day.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for any questions that sound closed or rushed. Read those lines again, turning them into open questions and adding a short pause at the end. Notice how the invitation feels warmer and more natural.
