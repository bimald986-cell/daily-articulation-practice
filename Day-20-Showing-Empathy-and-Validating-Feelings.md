# Day 20 – Showing Empathy and Validating Feelings

**Focus today:** Notice the feeling underneath the words and name it gently. Empathy shows that you care about the person, not only the problem. Validating feelings makes conversations safer and more human.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. Let your pace stay steady and your face open as you speak.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising showing empathy and validating feelings.

Understanding is powerful.

When I name what someone might be feeling, they feel less alone.

That builds trust faster than fixing or advising too quickly.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin with a simple idea.

People want to feel that their experience makes sense.

Then I practise a short validation.

Someone says: “I’m frustrated because the deadline keeps moving and I can’t plan.”

I respond: “That sounds frustrating — especially when you can’t plan around it.”

I say it once at normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, letting the words show that I heard the feeling.

The second version feels more caring.

A good validation does not dismiss or jump to solutions right away.

It simply shows that the feeling is understandable.

People relax when they know their reaction is allowed.

I keep my voice warm and steady.

I do not rush past the feeling.

I let it settle and then stay quiet for a moment.

If I notice I jumped straight to advice, I pause and return to the feeling.

No long apology. Just a clear validation and I continue.

For example: “It makes sense that this feels heavy right now… What would help most in this moment?”

The conversation stays kind and useful.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that validating feelings makes me look more present and trustworthy.

People trust speakers who can meet them in the emotion, not only the facts.

If my energy starts to fade, I soften my shoulders and lower my voice slightly as I name the feeling.

The same empathy can still feel genuine.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that showing empathy is a skill I can practise every day.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for any places where you moved straight to solutions. Read those lines again, adding one short validation of the feeling before continuing. Notice how the exchange feels warmer and safer.
