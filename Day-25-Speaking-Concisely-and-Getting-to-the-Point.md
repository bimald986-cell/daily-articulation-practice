# Day 25 – Speaking Concisely and Getting to the Point

**Focus today:** Say what matters in fewer, clearer words. Concise speech respects time and makes your message easier to remember.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. Let your pace stay steady and your face open as you speak.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising speaking concisely and getting to the point.

Clear messages land better when they are short and focused.

When I remove extra words, people hear the idea more easily.

That builds trust and keeps conversations moving.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin with a simple idea.

People stay with me longer when I do not wander.

Then I practise a short, direct message.

Someone asks for an update on the project.

I say: “The draft is ready. We need one more review before Friday, then we can send it.”

I say it once at normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, letting only the key facts remain.

The second version feels cleaner.

Good concise speech names the main point and the next step.

It leaves out background that is not needed right now.

People feel respected when their time is protected.

I keep my voice warm and steady.

I do not rush or add filler words.

I let the idea settle and then stay quiet for a moment.

If I notice I started explaining too much, I pause and return to the core.

No long apology. Just a clearer sentence and I continue.

For example: “The main risk is the timeline. Everything else is on track.”

The conversation stays useful and focused.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that speaking this way makes me look more confident and prepared.

People trust speakers who can say the important thing simply.

If my energy starts to fade, I soften my shoulders and keep my tone calm as I finish the thought.

The same message can still feel complete.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that speaking concisely is a skill I can practise every day.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for any places where you added extra words or background. Read those lines again, keeping only the main point and one next step. Notice how the message feels sharper and easier to follow.
