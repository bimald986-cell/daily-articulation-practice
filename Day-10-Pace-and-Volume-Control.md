# Day 10 – Pace & Volume Control

**Focus today:** Find a calm, natural pace. Speak at a volume that feels strong without forcing it. Let the speed and loudness serve the meaning.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Breathe. Notice when you want to speed up. Gently slow down instead. Let your volume stay steady and clear.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising pace and volume control.

My natural speaking speed is often a little too fast.

When I am excited or nervous, the words start to run.

Today I practise choosing the speed on purpose.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin slower than I think I need to.

Every sentence has room to land.

I do not race to the full stop.

I finish the thought, then I pause.

Pace is not about speaking as slowly as possible.

It is about matching the speed to the idea.

When I explain something important, I slow down a little.

When I tell a light story, I can move a little quicker.

The change itself keeps the listener interested.

I practise one sentence at two speeds.

“I am ready to contribute.”

First I say it at my normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, with a short pause before “contribute.”

The second version feels clearer and more sure.

Volume works the same way.

I do not need to be loud to be heard.

I need to be clear and consistent.

I speak as if the person is sitting across a small table from me.

Not shouting across a room.

Not whispering so only I can hear.

A steady, medium volume feels confident.

If I drop my voice at the end of a sentence, the meaning can get lost.

I practise keeping the volume even right to the full stop.

“This work matters to me.”

I say it once and notice if the last word fades.

Then I say it again and keep the energy all the way through.

The sentence feels complete.

In interviews this helps me.

I will not rush my answers.

I will not speak so quietly that the interviewer has to lean in.

I will choose a comfortable pace and a clear volume, then stay there.

If I feel myself speeding up, I take one breath and continue more slowly.

The listener is grateful for the space.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that when I slow down, I feel more in control.

When I keep my volume steady, I sound more certain.

Pace and volume are not tricks.

They are simply the decision to be easy to understand.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning to speak at a speed and volume that feel natural and strong.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for places where your pace suddenly quickened or your volume dropped at the end of a sentence. Read those lines again, deliberately slower and with even volume all the way to the full stop. Notice how much more confident and clear the same words become.
