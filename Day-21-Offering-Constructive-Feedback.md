# Day 21 – Offering Constructive Feedback

**Focus today:** Give feedback that helps the other person grow without making them feel small. Clear, kind, and specific feedback builds trust and improves results.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. Let your pace stay steady and your face open as you speak.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising offering constructive feedback.

Feedback is a gift when it is given with care.

When I focus on the behaviour and the impact, people can hear me better.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin with a simple idea.

People want to improve, and they want to feel respected while they do it.

Then I practise a short piece of feedback.

Someone’s presentation ran long and missed the key point.

I say: “The detail was useful. Next time, if we lead with the main recommendation, it will land more clearly.”

I say it once at normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, letting the words stay kind and specific.

The second version feels more helpful.

Good feedback names what worked and what can improve.

It stays focused on the work, not the person.

People feel safer when the feedback is clear and forward-looking.

I keep my voice warm and steady.

I do not rush or soften the point so much that it disappears.

I let the message settle and then stay quiet for a moment.

If I notice I started with what was wrong, I pause and begin again with something that worked.

No long apology. Just a clear, balanced note and I continue.

For example: “You prepared thoroughly. Adding a short summary at the start would help the group follow more easily.”

The conversation stays respectful and useful.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that giving feedback this way makes me look more confident and caring.

People trust speakers who can be honest without being harsh.

If my energy starts to fade, I soften my shoulders and keep my tone steady as I deliver the point.

The same feedback can still feel constructive.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that offering constructive feedback is a skill I can practise every day.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for any places where the feedback felt too vague or too sharp. Read those lines again, making one change more specific and one tone warmer. Notice how the feedback becomes easier to receive.
