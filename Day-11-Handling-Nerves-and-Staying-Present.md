# Day 11 – Handling Nerves and Staying Present

**Focus today:** Notice nerves without letting them take the wheel. Stay present with your breath and your words. Calm is a practice, not a personality trait.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. If your heart speeds up, that is fine. Keep speaking anyway.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising handling nerves and staying present.

Nerves are not a sign that something is wrong.

They are simply energy.

I can notice them and still choose how I speak.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

When I feel a flutter in my chest, I do not fight it.

I name it quietly: this is nerves.

Then I return to the next sentence.

The feeling can stay. My voice does not have to follow it.

I practise a short line while the feeling is present.

“I am glad to be here.”

I say it once at normal volume.

Then I take one breath and say it again, a little slower.

The second version feels steadier.

In interviews or conversations this helps me.

I do not need to wait until the nerves disappear.

I can start speaking while they are still there.

If my mind jumps ahead to what might go wrong, I come back to the current sentence.

One clear sentence is enough for this moment.

I do not need to be perfect.

I only need to be present.

When I pause, I stay with the pause instead of filling it quickly.

The silence is not dangerous.

It is simply space.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that the longer I stay, the smaller the nerves become.

They do not vanish completely.

They just stop running the show.

If my voice shakes a little, I keep going.

A small shake is still clear.

People hear the words more than the shake.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that nerves and presence can exist at the same time.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for moments where your voice tightened or sped up. Read those sentences again while deliberately softening your shoulders and taking one breath before each one. Notice that the same words feel calmer when your body is softer.
