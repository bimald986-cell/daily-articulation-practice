# Day 16 – Strong Openings and Clear Closings

**Focus today:** Begin with a clear, inviting opening and end with a strong, memorable close. A good start draws people in. A clear finish leaves them with something useful to take away.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. Let your pace stay steady and your face open as you speak.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising strong openings and clear closings.

The first few sentences set the tone.

They tell the listener whether to lean in or drift away.

I want them to lean in.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin with a simple, clear line.

“Today I want to share one idea that has helped me speak more clearly.”

I say it once at normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, letting the words land fully.

The second version feels more intentional.

A strong opening does not need to be clever.

It needs to be clear and warm.

People stay with me when they know what this conversation is about.

I keep my voice steady and open.

I do not rush the first sentence.

I let it settle.

Now I practise the close.

A clear closing brings the ideas together and points forward.

I say one short ending.

“The main thing I want to leave with you is this: clarity grows when we slow down and choose our words with care. Thank you for listening.”

I say it once.

Then I say it again, pausing briefly before “Thank you for listening.”

The pause gives the message room to land.

In conversations this helps me.

People remember the ending more than the middle.

A clear close makes the whole message feel complete.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that a strong opening and a clean finish make me look more prepared and confident.

People trust speakers who know how to begin and how to end.

If my energy starts to fade near the close, I soften my shoulders and lift my voice slightly on the final sentence.

The last words can still carry strength.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that openings and closings are skills I can practise every day.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen to the first two sentences and the last two sentences. Read the opening again, making sure it is clear and unhurried. Read the closing again, adding a short pause before the final thank-you. Notice how a stronger start and a cleaner finish change the feeling of the whole speech.
