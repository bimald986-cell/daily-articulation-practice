# Day 23 – Handling Disagreement with Respect

**Focus today:** Stay calm and curious when opinions differ. Disagreement handled with respect keeps relationships strong and ideas clear.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. Let your pace stay steady and your face open as you speak.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising handling disagreement with respect.

Difference of opinion is natural.

When I stay open and clear, people can hear each other better.

That builds trust even when we do not agree.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin with a simple idea.

People feel safer when disagreement does not become personal.

Then I practise a short response to disagreement.

Someone says: “I think we should delay the launch until next month.”

I respond: “I hear that you want more time. I see the risk of launching now. Can we look at both options together?”

I say it once at normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, letting the words stay calm and curious.

The second version feels more respectful.

Good disagreement names what I heard and what I see.

It invites dialogue instead of defending a position.

People stay open when the tone stays kind.

I keep my voice warm and steady.

I do not rush or raise my volume.

I let the idea settle and then stay quiet for a moment.

If I notice I started defending too quickly, I pause and return to what I heard.

No long apology. Just a clearer invitation and I continue.

For example: “You mentioned the extra testing. That matters. What part feels most important to protect?”

The conversation stays useful and respectful.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that handling disagreement this way makes me look more confident and grounded.

People trust speakers who can stay calm when views differ.

If my energy starts to fade, I soften my shoulders and keep my tone steady as I invite the next thought.

The same respect can still feel genuine.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that handling disagreement with respect is a skill I can practise every day.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for any places where the tone became sharp or defensive. Read those lines again, adding one acknowledgement of the other view before stating yours. Notice how the exchange feels calmer and more open.
