# Day 6 – Vocal Variety & Rhythm

**Focus today:** Bring colour to your voice. Change pace, pitch, and volume naturally. Let the rhythm match the meaning.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Speak slowly at first, then play with speed. Let your voice rise and fall. Smile when the words are warm.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising vocal variety and rhythm.

A clear voice is good.

A voice with colour is better.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

Some sentences need to be slow and steady.

Like this one.

I let each word land before the next one begins.

Other sentences can move a little faster when the idea is light.

They can pick up energy without losing clarity.

I do not speak at one speed the whole time.

I let the content decide the pace.

When I talk about something important, my voice drops slightly and slows down.

When I share something hopeful, my voice lifts and warms.

I do not force the changes.

I feel them first, then I let them show.

Listen to the difference.

“I am building a new life.”

That can be flat.

Or it can carry quiet strength if I say it lower and slower.

“I am excited about what comes next.”

That one can rise a little at the end and smile with the voice.

Variety keeps people listening.

It shows I care about the message.

It also shows I am comfortable with my own sound.

In interviews I will use this.

I will slow down when I describe my experience.

I will let warmth come through when I talk about working with people.

I will pause before the key point so it stands out.

Rhythm is not decoration.

It is how meaning travels.

If I rush everything, nothing feels important.

If I vary the pace, the listener knows where to lean in.

I practise the same sentence three ways.

First, very slowly and clearly.

Second, a little faster with more energy.

Third, soft and calm.

All three can be useful. The moment decides which one fits.

I look into my own eyes.

I do not look away when my voice changes.

I stay present.

If a sentence feels stiff, I breathe and try it again with a small smile.

Warmth is allowed.

Clarity and warmth together make people want to keep listening.

I finish this practice with a full breath.

I notice how my voice feels different now.

I am learning to give my words colour and rhythm.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Play it back and notice where your voice stayed on one level. Read those sentences again, deliberately changing the pace or pitch. Feel how much more alive the same words become.
