# Day 26 – Using Concrete Examples to Clarify Ideas

**Focus today:** Ground your ideas in a clear, specific example so people can picture them and remember them.

Read this out loud in front of the mirror. Stand tall. Soften your shoulders. Let your pace stay steady and your face open as you speak.

---

Hello.

Today I am practising using concrete examples to clarify ideas.

Abstract ideas become real when I show them in action.

A short example helps people see exactly what I mean.

I take a deep breath in through my nose… and I release it slowly through my mouth.

I stand tall. Feet planted. Shoulders soft. Eyes on the person in the mirror.

I begin with a simple idea.

People understand faster when they can picture the situation.

Then I practise adding one clear example.

Someone asks how we can improve team updates.

I say: “For example, last week Maya sent a three-line note after the meeting: what we decided, who owns the next step, and the date. That made the follow-up easy for everyone.”

I say it once at normal pace.

Then I say it again, a little slower, letting the details stay specific and vivid.

The second version feels clearer.

Good examples name a real moment, the action, and the result.

They stay short so the main point stays in focus.

People stay with me when the picture is simple.

I keep my voice warm and steady.

I do not rush through the details.

I let the example settle and then stay quiet for a moment.

If I notice the example is getting long, I pause and return to the core idea.

No long apology. Just a shorter version and I continue.

For example: “A short written note after the call keeps everyone aligned. That is the habit I want us to try.”

The conversation stays practical and useful.

I look into my own eyes.

I notice that using examples this way makes me look more prepared and helpful.

People trust speakers who make ideas easy to see.

If my energy starts to fade, I soften my shoulders and keep my tone clear as I finish the thought.

The same example can still feel concrete.

I finish this practice with one full breath.

I smile at the person in the mirror.

I am learning that using concrete examples is a skill I can practise every day.

One day at a time.

---

**Practice tip after reading:**  
Record yourself. Listen for any places where the example felt vague. Read those lines again, adding one specific person, action, and result. Notice how the idea becomes easier to picture and remember.
